Parts of Speech — Complete Identification System

📌 1. NOUN (Naming Word)

POS-01
WHAT IT DOES
Names a person, place, thing, idea, quality, or state.
HOW TO IDENTIFY
Ask the sentence: Who? What? Whom? (Nouns function as subject or object).
IDENTIFICATION TRICK
Words ending in -tion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -hood, -ship, -ance, -ence, -cy are almost always nouns.
TYPES
Proper (Ram, Delhi), Common (boy, city), Collective (team, jury), Material (gold, water), Abstract (honesty, love).
✅ EXAMPLE: Honesty is the best policy. (Honesty = Abstract Noun).
❌ COMMON TRAP: Treating abstract/uncountable nouns as countable by adding '-s' or 'a/an' (e.g. "an honesty", "informations").
📝 DRILL QUESTION
Find the error: "The committee (A) have reached (B) an unanimous decision (C) on the issue. (D)"

📌 2. PRONOUN (Noun Substitute)

POS-02
WHAT IT DOES
Replaces a noun to prevent repetitive mention.
HOW TO IDENTIFY
Check if it refers back to an antecedent noun (he, she, it, they, who, which, this, himself).
TYPES
Personal (I, You, He), Demonstrative (This, That), Relative (Who, Which, That), Reflexive (Myself, Himself), Indefinite (Someone, One).
✅ EXAMPLE: Ram said that he would finish the work himself.
❌ COMMON TRAP: Double noun/pronoun redundancy ("Ram, he is a good boy" ❌ → "Ram is a good boy" ✅).

📌 3. ADJECTIVE (Noun Qualifier)

POS-03
WHAT IT DOES
Qualifies or describes a noun or pronoun.
HOW TO IDENTIFY
Ask: Which one? What kind of? How many? How much?
DEGREES
Positive (tall), Comparative (taller), Superlative (tallest).
✅ EXAMPLE: She is a smart girl. Tea is preferable to coffee.
❌ COMMON TRAP: Double comparatives ("more taller" ❌ → "taller" ✅) or using 'than' with preferable/senior/superior ("preferable than" ❌ → "preferable to" ✅).

📌 4. VERB (Action & State Engine)

POS-04
WHAT IT DOES
Expresses action, state of being, or occurrence. The backbone of every sentence.
TRANSITIVE VS INTRANSITIVE
Transitive: Requires a direct object (e.g. He bought a car).
Intransitive: Does not take an object (e.g. Birds fly).
✅ EXAMPLE: She wrote a letter. / Water boils at 100°C.
❌ COMMON TRAP: Putting prepositions after transitive verbs in active voice ("discuss about the topic" ❌ → "discuss the topic" ✅).

📌 5. ADVERB (Action & Degree Qualifier)

POS-05
WHAT IT DOES
Modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb.
HOW TO IDENTIFY
Ask: How? When? Where? How often? To what extent? (Many end in -ly, e.g. quickly, very, seldom, hardly).
✅ EXAMPLE: He ran very fast. / Seldom does he come here. (Inversion).
❌ COMMON TRAP: Using adjectives instead of adverbs to modify verbs ("He works hard" ✅, "He speaks fluent" ❌ → "fluently" ✅).

📌 6. PREPOSITION (Relationship Marker)

POS-06
WHAT IT DOES
Shows relationship of position, direction, or time between a noun/pronoun and other words.
✅ EXAMPLE: The book is on the table. / She is good at English.
❌ COMMON TRAP: Saying "good in English" (Wrong ❌ → "good at English" ✅).

📌 7. CONJUNCTION (Sentence Connector)

POS-07
WHAT IT DOES
Connects words, phrases, or clauses.
TYPES
Coordinating (and, but, or), Subordinating (because, although, if, since), Correlative Pairs (either...or, no sooner...than).
✅ EXAMPLE: No sooner did he enter than the bell rang.

📌 8. INTERJECTION (Exclamatory Expresser)

POS-08
WHAT IT DOES
Expresses sudden emotion, pain, excitement, or surprise. Has no grammatical connection to the rest of the sentence.
✅ EXAMPLE: Alas! He failed the examination. / Bravo! You won the trophy.

Nouns & Numbers

🏷️ The "Always Singular" Uncountables

NOUN-01
RULE
Certain nouns in English are uncountable. They take a singular verb and CANNOT be made plural with 's' or 'es'.
TARGET WORDS
Furniture, advice, information, luggage, baggage, scenery, machinery, poetry, work, knowledge, traffic, percentage, garbage, chalk, bread, equipment, clothing, hair, alphabet, mischief, paper, crockery, cutlery, stationery, jewelry, dirt, dust.
✅ EXAMPLE: He gave me some advice. The scenery of Kashmir is beautiful.
❌ COMMON TRAP: Adding 's' to these words ("He gave me many advices" ❌).

🏷️ Numerical Expression Rule

NOUN-02
RULE
When a numerical expression acts as an adjective before a noun, it must stay in the SINGULAR form!
FORMULA
Number + Hyphenated Singular Noun + Main Noun
✅ EXAMPLE: He gave me a ten-rupee note. A five-year plan.
❌ COMMON TRAP: Pluralizing the adjective noun ("ten-rupees note" ❌).

🏷️ The Possessive Apostrophe Master Rules

NOUN-03
RULE 1 (HISSING SOUND)
Nouns ending in a hissing 's' sound take ONLY an apostrophe (') without adding another 's'.
RULE 2 (JOINT VS SEPARATE)
Joint Possession: Add apostrophe to the LAST noun only (*Ram and Shyam's shop* = 1 shop).
Separate Possession: Add apostrophe to BOTH nouns (*Ram's and Shyam's shops* = 2 shops).
RULE 3 (NON-LIVING TRAP)
Do NOT use `'s` with non-living things! Use "of the".
✅ EXAMPLES:
• Keats' poems are sublime. (Hissing sound).
• Ram and Shyam's shop is closed. (Joint).
• The leg of the table is broken. (Non-living).

🏷️ Collective Nouns — Identification & Behaviour

NOUN-04
DEFINITION
A single word that names a GROUP of people/things acting as one unit (family, team, army, jury, committee, crowd, herd, flock, class).
RULE
Takes a SINGULAR verb when the group acts as one unit; takes a PLURAL verb when members act individually/separately.
✅ EXAMPLE: The team is playing well (as one unit). / The team are arguing among themselves (as individuals).

Pronouns

👤 Order of Personal Pronouns (2-3-1 vs 1-2-3)

PRO-01
RULE 1 (NORMAL)
For general/positive sentences: 2nd Person → 3rd Person → 1st Person (2 - 3 - 1).
RULE 2 (CONFESSION)
For mistakes, confessions, bad deeds, or plural pronouns: 1st Person → 2nd Person → 3rd Person (1 - 2 - 3).
✅ NORMAL (2-3-1): You, he and I will study together.
✅ CONFESSION (1-2-3): I, you and he committed the crime.

👤 Relative Pronouns: Who vs. Whom

PRO-02
RULE
Who is a subject pronoun (replaces he/she/they). Whom is an object pronoun (replaces him/her/them).
✅ EXAMPLE: The man who called you is here. (He called). / The man whom you called is here. (You called him).

👤 The "One and One's" Rule

PRO-03
RULE
When 'One' is used as a subject, its possessive MUST be ONE'S, never 'his' or 'their'.
✅ EXAMPLE: One must do one's duty.
❌ COMMON TRAP: "One must do his duty." (Wrong ❌ -> one's duty ✅).

👤 Reflexive Pronoun Mandate Verbs

PRO-04
RULE
Specific transitive verbs MUST take a reflexive pronoun (*myself, himself, themselves*) when NO direct object follows them in the sentence!
TARGET VERBS
Enjoy, Avail, Absent, Adapt, Introduce, Resign, Exert, Pride, Reconcile, Apply.
✅ EXAMPLE: He absented himself from the class yesterday. / She availed herself of the opportunity.
❌ COMMON TRAP: Omitting the reflexive pronoun.
"He absented from the class." (Wrong ❌ -> absented himself ✅).

👤 Nominative Case After "Be" Verbs

PRO-05
RULE
When a pronoun follows a form of the verb 'to be' (is, am, are, was, were, be, been) as a complement, it MUST be in the NOMINATIVE (Subjective) CASE!
✅ EXAMPLE: It is I who called you. / If I were he, I would accept the offer.
❌ COMMON TRAP: "It is me who called you." (Wrong ❌ -> "It is I" ✅).

👤 Each Other vs. One Another

PRO-07
RULE
Each other is used for TWO people/things. One another is used for MORE THAN TWO (traditionally tested this way in exams).
✅ EXAMPLE: The two brothers help each other. / All five members respect one another.

👤 Possessive vs. Contraction Traps (Its/It's, Whose/Who's, Your/You're)

PRO-08
RULE
Possessive pronouns (its, whose, your, their) NEVER take an apostrophe. The apostrophe forms are always contractions (it is, who is, you are, they are).
✅ EXAMPLE: The dog wagged its tail. (possession) / It's raining today. (It is).
❌ COMMON TRAP: "The dog wagged it's tail." (Wrong ❌ → its ✅).

👤 Pronoun–Antecedent Agreement

PRO-09
RULE
A pronoun MUST agree with its antecedent (the noun it replaces) in NUMBER (singular/plural) and GENDER, and the reference must be unambiguous (only one possible noun it could refer to).
✅ EXAMPLE: Every girl must bring her ID card.
❌ COMMON TRAP: "Ravi told Suresh that he had passed." — unclear whether "he" is Ravi or Suresh (ambiguous reference).

👤 Who vs. Whom — The He/Him Substitution Test

PRO-10
RULE
Who = Subject case (does the action). Whom = Object case (receives the action).
IDENTIFY IT
Mentally answer the question with He/Him. If "He" fits → use WHO. If "Him" fits → use WHOM.
✅ EXAMPLE: Who is calling? (He is calling → WHO). Whom did you meet? (I met him → WHOM).

👤 "One" as an Impersonal Pronoun — Consistency Rule

PRO-11
RULE
Once a sentence begins with the formal impersonal pronoun "One", it must be referred back to consistently as "one" or "one's" — NOT switched to he/his or you/your midway.
✅ EXAMPLE: One should love one's country.
❌ COMMON TRAP: "One should love his country." (Wrong ❌ — inconsistent shift from 'one' to 'his').

👤 Double Subject (Redundant Pronoun) Error

PRO-12
RULE
Do NOT repeat the subject as a pronoun immediately after stating the noun subject — this is a redundant "double subject" error.
✅ EXAMPLE: My father is a doctor.
❌ COMMON TRAP: "My father he is a doctor." (Wrong ❌ — remove the redundant "he").

🔍 A vs. An — The Real Rule Is SOUND, Not Spelling

ART-02
RULE
Use "an" before a word that STARTS WITH A VOWEL SOUND. Use "a" before a word that STARTS WITH A CONSONANT SOUND — regardless of the actual first letter.
IDENTIFY IT
Say the word aloud. If it opens with an /a,e,i,o,u/ sound → an. If it opens with a /y,w,h/-glide or consonant sound → a.

✅ 'U/EU/O' SOUNDING LIKE 'YOU' → A: a university, a useful tool, a European, a one-rupee coin, a one-eyed man.
✅ ABBREVIATIONS BY SOUND: an MBA, an M.P., an SSC officer (S sounds like "ess"), a B.A., a University degree.
❌ COMMON TRAP: "an university" ❌ (sounds like "yoo-") → a university ✅. "a hour" ❌ (h is silent) → an hour ✅.

🔍 Another vs. Other vs. The Other

ART-03

OTHER

THE OTHER

✅ EXAMPLE: Give me another pen. / Other students left early. / I have two pens; the other one is red.

✅ EXAMPLE: We have enough time. / He is tall enough to reach the shelf.
❌ COMMON TRAP: "He is enough tall." (Wrong ❌ → "tall enough" ✅).

RULE

RULE

TENSE-03
RULE

✅ EXAMPLE: The teacher told us that the sun rises in the east. (Universal truth — no backshift).
❌ COMMON TRAP: "The teacher told us that the sun rose in the east." (Wrong ❌ — universal truths stay present).

Parallelism Rules

⏸️ Rule of Parallel Structure

PAR-01
RULE
Items in a series or joined by conjunctions (and, or, but, as well as) MUST have identical grammatical form (all gerunds, all infinitives, or all adjectives).
✅ EXAMPLE: He likes swimming, dancing, and reading.
❌ COMMON TRAP: "He likes swimming, dancing, and to read." (Wrong ❌ -> reading ✅).

⏸️ Parallelism With Correlative Conjunctions

PAR-02
RULE
Whatever grammatical structure (word, phrase, clause) follows the FIRST part of a correlative pair (not only, both, either, neither) MUST be matched by the same structure after the SECOND part.
HOW TO IDENTIFY
Underline what comes right after each half of the pair — a noun must match a noun, a verb phrase must match a verb phrase.
✅ EXAMPLE: He is not only intelligent but also hardworking. (adjective ↔ adjective)
❌ COMMON TRAP: "He not only is intelligent but also hardworking." (Wrong ❌ — verb "is" placed before "not only" breaks the balance) → "He is not only intelligent but also hardworking" ✅.

⏸️ Parallelism in Comparisons (as...as / than)

PAR-03
RULE
When comparing two things with "as...as" or "than", both sides of the comparison must be the SAME grammatical category (noun vs noun, gerund vs gerund) — you cannot compare a thing to an action.
✅ EXAMPLE: Reading a book is more useful than watching TV.
❌ COMMON TRAP: "The population of Delhi is more than Mumbai." (Wrong ❌ — comparing population to a city) → "The population of Delhi is more than that of Mumbai" ✅.

⏸️ Parallel Prepositions in a List

PAR-04
RULE
When items in a list share the same preposition, you may drop the repeated preposition. But if different items need DIFFERENT prepositions, each preposition must be stated explicitly.
✅ EXAMPLE: He has both interest in and knowledge of music. (different prepositions — both required)
❌ COMMON TRAP: "He has interest and knowledge of music." (Wrong ❌ — "interest of" is incorrect) → "interest in and knowledge of" ✅.
